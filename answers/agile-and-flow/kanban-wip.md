# How do Kanban and WIP limits help a team finish work?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#agile-scrum-and-kanban)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Agile, Scrum, and Kanban · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Kanban makes workflow, policies, and work in progress visible so the team can manage flow. Agree what started and finished mean, how work is selected, and how blocked items are handled.

If five items are waiting for one reviewer, starting more development increases the queue. A WIP limit encourages helping complete or unblock existing work before starting another item. Treat exceptions explicitly and inspect their effect. A board with unlimited hidden work provides little control. WIP limits should be adapted using flow evidence and team discussion; they are not a quota for measuring individual busyness.

## Choose a response to a full queue

Suppose development can start eight items but review can finish only three in the same period. More starts will build waiting work unless the team changes the constraint. Help review, clarify acceptance earlier, split items, or reduce new starts.

Define whether blocked items count against the WIP policy; hiding them outside the board distorts the picture. An urgent exception should name the reason and the work it displaces.

A WIP control supports a pull system: new work is selected when there is capacity under the workflow policy. It does not require one universal limit or guarantee shorter cycle time by itself. Inspect completion, age, quality, and the effect of the chosen policy.
