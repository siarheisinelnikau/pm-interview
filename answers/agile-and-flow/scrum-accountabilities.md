# Who owns what in Scrum, and where can a Project Manager contribute?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#agile-scrum-and-kanban)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Agile, Scrum, and Kanban · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Scrum has three accountabilities: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. The Product Owner is accountable for product value and effective Product Backlog management. Developers plan and adapt their work. The Scrum Master establishes Scrum and supports the team's effectiveness.

A PM working alongside the team can coordinate contracts, external dependencies, funding, and organizational reporting. Agree those boundaries explicitly. If a sponsor requests new work, route the priority discussion through the Product Owner and let Developers assess the plan. A separate title does not grant authority to assign Sprint work or override the team's quality commitments.

## Explain a boundary conflict

If a PM promises five Sprint items before Developers plan them, resolve the promise as an external constraint and delivery risk. Discuss priority with the Product Owner and feasibility with Developers; do not turn the promise into an assignment mechanism.

The Product Owner may delegate backlog activities but remains accountable. The Scrum Master helps people understand and use Scrum, and is accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness. Developers include the people creating the Increment, not only people whose job title is programmer.

A person holding multiple roles must still preserve these accountabilities. An interview answer should explain how coordination works in that organization without adding a command hierarchy to Scrum's self-management.
