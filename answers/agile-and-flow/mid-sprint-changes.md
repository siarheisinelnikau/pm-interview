# How should a Scrum team respond to urgent work during a Sprint?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#agile-scrum-and-kanban)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Agile, Scrum, and Kanban · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Clarify urgency, user impact, and whether the work threatens the Sprint Goal. Developers and the Product Owner can negotiate scope as more is learned, while preserving the goal and quality expectations. Only the Product Owner has authority to cancel a Sprint when its goal becomes obsolete.

For a genuine production incident, use the agreed incident process and make the interruption visible. Reforecast affected delivery and review whether repeated interruptions need a different capacity or workflow policy. Do not silently add work while holding the original forecast fixed. Also avoid declaring all stakeholder requests emergencies merely because they arrive from senior people.

## Make the cost of interruption visible

For an urgent defect, establish affected users and impact first. If one Developer joins incident response, the rest of the team and Product Owner inspect what that means for the Sprint Goal and forecast. Record displaced work rather than counting the interruption as free capacity.

If a sponsor simply wants a new feature sooner, compare urgency and negotiate the Product Backlog order. A PM should not independently cancel the Sprint or require quality shortcuts.

Repeated operational interruptions may justify explicit support capacity, smaller batches, or a different workflow policy. Changing the operating model requires discussion; repeatedly missing the same forecast without learning is not adaptation.
