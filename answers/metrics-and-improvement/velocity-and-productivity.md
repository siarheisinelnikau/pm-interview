# Why is velocity a poor individual or cross-team performance metric?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#metrics-and-continuous-improvement)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Metrics and Continuous Improvement · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Velocity reflects completed work in a team's local estimation system over a period. It can help the team forecast comparable work, but points do not form a standardized unit of productivity. Changes in slicing, estimation, quality, or team composition affect the number.

Ranking people or teams by points rewards inflation and hides collaboration. If velocity rises, inspect whether more useful work is actually reaching users without additional defects or overload. For improvement, examine waiting, blocked work, quality, and outcomes with the team. Treat every metric as evidence to interpret, not a target that replaces judgment.

## Challenge a misleading improvement

A team completes 20 points, then 40 after changing its estimation scale. No additional output has necessarily occurred. Even with a stable scale, more points may reflect larger batches, deferred testing, or work that does not help users.

Use a team's history only while its work, estimation, and capacity remain sufficiently comparable. Velocity is not a required Scrum metric or a contractual unit of value.

If predictability is poor, inspect item size, interruptions, dependencies, and completion quality. Pair flow evidence with outcome and team-health information. Ask what changed in the delivery system before interpreting a higher number as improvement.
