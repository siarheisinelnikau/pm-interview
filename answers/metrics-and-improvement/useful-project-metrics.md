# Which metrics belong in a project dashboard?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#metrics-and-continuous-improvement)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Metrics and Continuous Improvement · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Choose measures that support actual decisions: forecast versus target, accepted scope, cost outlook, major risk exposure, dependency readiness, quality, and user or business outcomes. Define data sources, boundaries, owners, and update frequency.

Use a small balanced set. A project can improve output while increasing incidents or failing to achieve adoption. Pair delivery progress with evidence of quality and the intended outcome. Explain variance and the action it calls for rather than presenting charts without decisions. Remove a metric if nobody can explain what they would do differently when it changes.

## Connect a metric to a response

For each metric, state the question and the decision it supports. Rising aged work may call for unblocking rather than new starts. A worsening cost forecast may require scope or funding decisions. Low adoption may require workflow investigation rather than faster development.

Include denominators and segmentation. Ten failures out of 100 attempts has a different meaning from ten out of 100,000; aggregate success can hide one failing site or user group.

Maintain a small metric dictionary with owner, source, clock, boundary, and update cadence. Inspect both leading signals and realized outcomes. A dashboard should make a conversation more accurate, not replace the investigation behind it.
