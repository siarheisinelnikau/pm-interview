# How does a product roadmap differ from a project plan?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#product-management---adjacent)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Product Management - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

A roadmap communicates product direction and intended outcomes over time, with uncertainty appropriate to the horizon. A project plan coordinates scope, work, resources, dependencies, milestones, and control for an initiative.

Connect them without treating every future roadmap item as a dated commitment. For example, a roadmap outcome around onboarding can lead to discovery and several delivery increments. Use evidence to refine scope and forecasts, and explain separately which dates are targets, forecasts, or authorized commitments.

## Keep the horizons consistent

A roadmap might say “improve onboarding completion next quarter.” Discovery could lead to a two-stage project plan with integration, verification, rollout, and an outcome review.

Show nearer-term work in more detail and future options with appropriate uncertainty. If a customer agreement requires a specific delivery date, represent it as a commitment and its assumptions in the project plan. Do not let the roadmap silently acquire contractual meaning.

At review, explain whether new evidence changed the intended outcome, the chosen solution, or only its delivery forecast. Those are different decisions and may have different owners.
