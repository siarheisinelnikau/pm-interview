# How does a Project Manager support product discovery?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#product-management---adjacent)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Product Management - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Support access to users, data, specialists, and decision makers. Make discovery uncertainty visible in plans and budgets. The product owner of the decision should define the problem, intended outcome, and what evidence could change direction.

A bounded discovery may conclude that an existing process or smaller change solves the problem. Record that result and its implications before committing to a build. The PM coordinates time, dependencies, and follow-through; product accountability includes deciding which problem is worth solving and what outcome to pursue.

## Make the next decision explicit

For a referral workflow, discovery might compare process changes, an integration, and a new product capability. Agree what evidence would justify each, including user benefit, feasibility, and operational risk.

The PM can timebox access to clinicians and technical specialists, track dependencies, and make the cost of further investigation visible. Product judgment determines whether to proceed, pivot, or stop. A decision to avoid an unnecessary build can be a successful discovery result.

Preserve the learning in the delivery handover so assumptions do not quietly become facts. Use a short problem statement, evidence summary, unresolved questions, and next investment decision.
