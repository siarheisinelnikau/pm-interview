# A client-owned dependency will miss a milestone. What do you do?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#risk-and-issue-management)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Risk and Issue Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Confirm the facts with the dependency owner and assess the effect on the current forecast. Agree immediate actions and a realistic revised availability date. Explore resequencing, substitute data or interfaces, reduced scope, and a milestone change.

Escalate early enough for a decision: state the impact, options, recommendation, accountable owner, and latest useful decision time. Record relevant contractual assumptions through the agreed change process. Keep collaboration practical rather than assigning blame. Once the decision is made, update all affected plans and inspect whether the workaround introduces quality or integration risk that needs separate verification.

## Escalate before the fallback expires

Suppose client access is due Friday, but an alternative test arrangement needs three days to establish. Set the decision time early enough to preserve that option. Ask the client owner whether recovery is credible and what evidence will confirm it.

Present: “Access is still unavailable; this affects integrated verification. Option A moves the milestone, option B funds an alternative with these limitations, and option C narrows scope.” Recommend one and name the authority needed.

After the decision, distinguish provisional test evidence from final evidence against the actual client environment. A workaround can reduce schedule impact while leaving a real integration obligation outstanding.
