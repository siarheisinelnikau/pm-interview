# How do mitigation, contingency plans, and reserves differ?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#risk-and-issue-management)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Risk and Issue Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Mitigation changes exposure before a risk materializes. A contingency plan describes what to do if a defined trigger occurs. A reserve provides approved time or money for uncertainty under specified control.

For a migration, early rehearsal is mitigation. Switching to a fallback window after a failed reconciliation is contingency. Allocated funding for the additional window is a reserve. Define release authority and review rules, including the organization's terminology for contingency and management reserves. Avoid hiding arbitrary padding in every estimate; it makes uncertainty harder to inspect and can double-count protection.

## Allocate the control explicitly

A migration estimate might include the planned rehearsal, while a separate approved reserve funds a second cutover attempt if a trigger occurs. Clarify whether that reserve is inside the performance baseline and who releases it.

Frameworks use “contingency” and “management reserve” differently. In many PMI-style explanations, identified-risk contingency is within the cost baseline and management reserve sits outside it; other public-sector frameworks allocate these terms differently. State the convention rather than applying a memorized label blindly.

Avoid adding the full same risk exposure to every task, a project buffer, and a sponsor reserve. Review remaining exposure and available protection together after major decisions.
