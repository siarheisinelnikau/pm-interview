# What is the difference between a risk, an issue, an assumption, and a dependency?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#risk-and-issue-management)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Risk and Issue Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

A risk is an uncertain event or condition that could affect objectives. An issue is a current problem requiring action. An assumption is a statement treated as true until validated. A dependency is something needed from another activity or party.

Example: sandbox access needed next week is a dependency; believing it will include realistic data is an assumption. The possibility of delayed access is a risk. Failure to receive access by the agreed date is an issue. Link these records as the situation evolves, with owners and next actions. One undifferentiated list makes escalation and forecasting harder.

## Watch the state change

If access arrives late, the uncertainty about lateness has materialized. Manage the present blocker as an issue, while retaining the linked risk history and any remaining uncertainty about recovery.

Each category calls for a different action: assess and respond to a risk, resolve or contain an issue, validate an assumption, and obtain or renegotiate a dependency. One event may need several linked records, but avoid duplicating ownership.

At a RAID review, ask what changed and what decision is needed. A risk with no trigger or response and an issue with no next action are both weak records. The purpose is earlier action, not a larger spreadsheet.
