# How do you choose between avoiding, mitigating, transferring, and accepting a risk?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#risk-and-issue-management)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Risk and Issue Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Avoid a threat by changing the plan so the exposure no longer applies. Mitigate its likelihood or impact. Transfer some consequences through an appropriate arrangement, while understanding remaining obligations. Accept a risk explicitly when the responsible authority judges it tolerable.

For an unstable vendor API, options could include using another provider, testing early with fallbacks, allocating contractual responsibilities, or proceeding with a documented contingency. Contracts do not eliminate operational or reputational impact. Record the residual risk and its owner. Revisit acceptance when the exposure changes; silence and an expired action are not informed acceptance.

## Include positive uncertainty

Threat responses reduce downside. Opportunities can instead be exploited, enhanced, shared, or accepted using the organization's terminology. For example, a partner's early certification slot may let the team release sooner if test readiness can be accelerated safely.

For threats, distinguish action ownership from acceptance authority. Engineering may implement a control, while a business risk owner decides whether the residual exposure is tolerable. Applicable legal duties still have to be met.

Compare response cost with the exposure and feasibility. Buying insurance may transfer some financial consequence, but service recovery, patient impact, and reputation can remain with the organization. Check what the arrangement actually covers before claiming the risk was transferred.
