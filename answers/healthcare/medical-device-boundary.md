# When might healthcare software require a medical-device assessment?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#healthcare-domain)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Healthcare Domain · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Assess **the software function and its intended purpose in each market**. Administrative software, information display, diagnosis, and treatment recommendations can have different regulatory positions even inside one application.

## Distinguish qualification from classification

Qualification asks whether a function falls within the relevant device framework. Classification asks which risk class and controls apply after it qualifies. “All other software is Class I” in a classification rule does not mean all ordinary software becomes a medical device.

In the US, assess the device definition and relevant exclusions or policies with the regulatory owner. FDA's final Clinical Decision Support guidance dated **January 29, 2026** supersedes its January 6, 2026 version. A claimed non-device CDS exclusion requires all applicable statutory criteria; the presence of a clinician alone does not establish it. Independent review of the recommendation's basis is one important consideration.

In the EU, assess MDR or IVDR qualification and applicable classification rules. MDCG 2019-11 Rev. 1, June 2025, explains software assessment including MDR Rule 11. The guidance is not itself binding law, and an example classification is not a determination for every similar-looking product.

## Translate the assessment into delivery

Record intended use, users, population, claims, markets, function boundaries, classification rationale, evidence strategy, and decision owner. Schedule quality-system work, verification, clinical evidence, external assessment or submission where required, and postmarket responsibilities.

For applicable US device manufacturers, QMSR became effective on **February 2, 2026**, incorporating ISO 13485:2016 by reference within FDA's regulatory framework. Do not describe that transition as still future. FDA's **February 2026** premarket cybersecurity guidance supersedes its June 2025 edition.

## Example

A product initially scheduling visits adds a function interpreting patient inputs to recommend treatment. Reassess the new function, claims, clinical evidence, and market obligations before carrying the old schedule and approval assumptions forward.

The PM coordinates the consequences; qualified regulatory and quality owners establish the applicable route. Neither an “AI” label nor a disclaimer alone settles the boundary.
