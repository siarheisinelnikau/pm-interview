# How do you break down scope into work that can be estimated and tracked?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#planning-and-estimation)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Planning and Estimation · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Start with deliverables or user outcomes, then decompose enough to identify ownership, dependencies, acceptance evidence, and estimation uncertainty. Keep scope coverage visible: testing, migration, training, security reviews, and transition are work too.

For an onboarding feature, a useful slice may let one eligible customer complete a full journey. Separate investigation when a vendor API is unknown. Avoid a plan consisting only of technical activities that cannot demonstrate progress to a stakeholder. Decompose further when an item is too large to discuss meaningfully, not merely to fill a template with hundreds of tasks.

## Decompose scope without losing the outcome

A WBS groups the full agreed scope into manageable components; a schedule adds sequence and dates. A product backlog orders evolving work. These can connect without being interchangeable.

For an onboarding release, include identity integration, exception handling, verification, training, and transition. Then identify a small vertical slice that demonstrates the full journey for one customer type. That slice may cross several technical components.

Use a completion rule for each component and check that their combined coverage represents the agreed scope without gaps or double counting. Decomposition should expose uncertainty and responsibility. Breaking one uncertain activity into twenty equally uncertain subtasks creates apparent precision rather than a better estimate.
