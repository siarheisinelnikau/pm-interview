# A fixed launch date is at risk. How would you build a recovery plan?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#planning-and-estimation)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Planning and Estimation · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Verify the current state using completed, accepted work and remaining dependencies. Identify the paths driving the delay and the consequences of missing the date. Build options with owners, cost, risks, and realistic outcome ranges.

Options might include releasing a smaller usable scope, moving the date, changing an integration approach, or adding targeted specialist support. Fast-tracking dependent work can increase rework; extra people do not instantly accelerate a team. Present a recommendation and a decision deadline to the sponsor. Rebaseline only after approval and keep the original variance visible. Track whether the recovery actions actually improve the forecast.

## Present options a sponsor can choose

| Option | Illustrative consequence | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep date, narrow scope | One customer segment moves later | Smaller scope still useful and safe |
| Keep scope, move date | More commercial delay | Updated dependency and capacity forecast |
| Add targeted help | Extra cost and onboarding | A specific constrained task can benefit |
| Overlap activities | Potentially shorter duration | Rework and verification risks understood |

State which option you recommend and why. A recovery plan needs a near-term checkpoint: for example, a tested integration by Friday that must occur for the revised forecast to remain credible.

Rebaselining records an authorized new reference. It must not erase why the original commitment was missed or obscure costs incurred during recovery.
