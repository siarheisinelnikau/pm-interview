# How do estimates, forecasts, targets, and commitments differ?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#planning-and-estimation)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Planning and Estimation · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

An estimate is an assessment of effort, duration, or cost with assumptions. A forecast predicts an outcome using current evidence. A target expresses a desired result. A commitment is a promise made by an authorized party.

If a client wants June 30, record it as a target until scope, capacity, dependencies, and acceptable uncertainty have been assessed. Present a forecast range and the choices that could improve it. If management commits to the date, preserve the underlying assumptions and decision record. Renaming an uncertain estimate as a commitment does not remove the uncertainty or create delivery capacity.

## State the distinction aloud

A useful message is: “June 30 is the target. Our current forecast is late June to mid-July, conditional on partner certification. We can recommend a commitment once we choose the initial scope and confirm that dependency.”

Separate effort from duration. Ten person-days of effort do not imply two calendar days when five people are assigned: work may be sequential, people may lack the same skills, and approval time may dominate.

Update the forecast when evidence changes and show its variance from the original baseline. A commitment creates an obligation to act and communicate responsibly; it does not make an uncertain estimate certain. Escalate the trade-off before the last useful decision date.
