# How would you improve delivery across several teams?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#delivery-management---adjacent)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Delivery Management - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Map the path from request to usable outcome, including queues, shared specialists, approvals, and operational handoffs. Identify the constraint affecting overall completion and change that before optimizing local busyness.

![A stacked area chart of done, review, development and not-started items totals twenty each day. Review accumulation warrants investigation.](../../assets/diagrams/cumulative-flow.svg)

Agree clear interfaces, shared integration evidence, and dependency ownership. Trial a small improvement, such as reducing batch size or reserving review capacity, then inspect end-to-end results and quality. A team finishing its own tickets faster can still increase the queue for another team.

## Inspect waiting across the boundary

Suppose one team builds six items weekly but a shared verification group completes three. Starting more development grows the queue. Check item comparability, rework, and the real constraint before changing staffing.

Options include reducing batch size, improving testability, cross-training, reserved integration windows, or lowering intake. Give the improvement an owner and measure end-to-end completion, waiting, and defects.

A shared milestone needs shared acceptance evidence. Each team's component being “done” does not prove the integrated user journey works. Local utilization or ticket completion should not hide unfinished work at handoffs.
