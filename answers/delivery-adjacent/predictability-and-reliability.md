# How would you balance delivery predictability, speed, and service reliability?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#delivery-management---adjacent)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Delivery Management - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Create a shared picture of flow, quality, interruptions, and service health. Forecast using representative evidence and protect capacity for necessary operational work. Agree how user impact or exhausted reliability tolerance changes priorities.

Investigate causes of delay rather than demanding every team increase output. Smaller changes, clearer acceptance, faster feedback, or reduced waiting may improve both speed and reliability. Report trade-offs and uncertainty explicitly. A predictable schedule achieved by hiding defects or deferring essential response work is not a healthy delivery result.

## Use a joint policy

Agree what happens when interruptions rise or reliability targets are threatened: review exposure, reduce intake where appropriate, protect response capacity, and reforecast affected work.

For a service with frequent deployment failures, a smaller change and a better rollback path may improve recovery and predictability together. Investigate the cause rather than assuming speed and stability always require opposing choices.

Use service-level evidence over time and distinguish a desired target from the current forecast. Communicate displaced work to product and business owners. Sustainable predictability depends on visible demand and realistic capacity, including the work needed to keep the service usable.
