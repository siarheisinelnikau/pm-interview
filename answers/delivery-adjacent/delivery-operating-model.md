# What lightweight operating model would you establish for delivery?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#delivery-management---adjacent)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Delivery Management - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Define intake and prioritization ownership, workflow policies, planning horizons, dependency review, decision thresholds, and operational feedback. Use a small set of shared measures and keep improvement ownership explicit.

Choose meetings and artifacts according to the coordination needed. A weekly dependency conversation and a clear escalation path may help more than an additional status layer. Revisit the model with teams after a trial. The goal is faster useful decisions and completed outcomes; copying another organization's ceremonies does not establish that the model fits yours.

## Start with a minimal set of agreements

A practical model might have one visible intake, a clear priority owner, explicit WIP policies, weekly dependency decisions, and a defined incident path. Link accepted work, readiness evidence, and improvement actions.

Name the owner and expected result of every recurring interaction. Keep a single factual view while allowing audiences different levels of detail. Tools such as a board or dashboard support these agreements but do not create authority by themselves.

Trial the model, inspect delays and decision quality, and remove steps that do not help. Adapt the cadence to demand and risk rather than applying one meeting structure everywhere.
