# How would you select and manage a supplier beyond comparing hourly rates?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#budget-and-commercial-management)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Budget and Commercial Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Evaluate capability, domain fit, delivery evidence, integration effort, security, continuity, support, and total cost. Check access to critical people and the ability to exit or transfer work. Agree observable deliverables and acceptance conditions before starting.

For an identity vendor, include test environments, migration, incident response, data handling, and subcontractors in the decision. Track dependencies and supplier risks in the overall project plan. Review progress through working evidence rather than only invoices or slide decks. A cheap supplier can increase total cost if onboarding, coordination, rework, or lock-in is ignored.

## Evaluate exit as well as entry

Ask a shortlisted supplier to demonstrate the relevant integration and exception path. Obtain references or evidence for comparable constraints. Use a total-cost view covering onboarding, recurring fees, data transfer, support, and exit effort.

Agree service contacts, subcontractor visibility, incident escalation, deliverable ownership, and how data and knowledge can be transferred. For a critical identity service, an outage or unavailable export can become a project-wide dependency.

During delivery, compare working evidence with obligations and forecast. If a supplier slips, agree recovery and assess alternatives early enough to use them. A penalty clause can address a commercial consequence while leaving operational harm unresolved.
