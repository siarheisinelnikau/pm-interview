# How do fixed-price, time-and-materials, and outcome-based arrangements affect project management?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#budget-and-commercial-management)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Budget and Commercial Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

These arrangements allocate commercial uncertainty differently, subject to the actual contract. Fixed-price work needs clear scope, assumptions, acceptance, and change handling. Time-and-materials needs visible spending, rates, capacity, and progress. Outcome-based work needs measurable results, attribution, and agreement on factors outside either party's control.

For an uncertain legacy integration, propose bounded discovery before pricing implementation. Review payment milestones, client dependencies, warranty, support, and exit conditions with the commercial owner. Do not assume a label decides every responsibility. A fixed price does not make ambiguous requirements stable, and billing hours does not prove useful progress.

## Match the control to the arrangement

| Model | Main delivery question | Practical control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed price | What result and boundary were actually agreed? | Acceptance evidence, assumptions, controlled change |
| Time and materials | Is spend producing useful accepted progress? | Rates, capacity, burn forecast, review points |
| Outcome based | Can the result be measured and attributed fairly? | Baseline, measurement rules, dependencies, exclusions |

For a US federal example, FAR defines particular fixed-price and T&M types; those rules are not universal commercial law. In any setting, read the signed agreement.

Include client response times, environments, data, and acceptance windows in the delivery assessment. The commercial owner decides how obligations and pricing are negotiated; the PM provides feasible delivery evidence.
