# How would you compare building, buying, and reusing a solution?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#budget-and-commercial-management)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Budget and Commercial Management · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Compare options against the business need, constraints, and whole-life consequences. Include adapting an existing capability and changing the process, rather than treating a new build and a new vendor as the only choices.

## Compare the same scope

For each option, assess functional fit, integration and migration, delivery uncertainty, assurance, operating model, accessibility, support, and ability to change or exit. Include licenses, implementation, recurring usage, internal ownership, supplier management, and decommissioning in total cost.

A vendor demonstration may exclude the difficult integration and exception paths. An internal estimate may omit maintenance and support. Make those omissions visible before comparing prices.

## Example: identity verification

A purchased service might accelerate access to a capability but introduce data handling, country coverage, review-queue, availability, and supplier dependencies. Building may provide more control while requiring specialist skills, continuing policy changes, and ongoing operations. Reusing a corporate service may be quickest if its owners can support the required population and load.

Use a bounded investigation or POC for uncertainty that can change the decision. Agree what evidence would disqualify an option.

## Decide with the right owners

Product or business owners assess fit and outcomes; engineering assesses feasibility; operations assesses support; security/privacy assess exposure; finance and commercial owners assess economics and obligations. The PM combines their evidence into options and a realistic plan.

Record why the chosen option fits now, its assumptions, and what would trigger reconsideration. A purchase does not remove delivery responsibilities, and an internal build does not remove third-party dependencies.
