# How would you handle repeated missed commitments by a team member?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#leadership-and-behavioral-questions)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Leadership and Behavioral Questions · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Talk privately using specific observations and their impact. Explore unclear expectations, workload, skills, external blockers, or personal circumstances without assuming intent. Agree a practical improvement, support, and review date.

If a specialist misses review windows, check whether several projects have allocated the same time. Adjust commitments or resolve allocation before treating the problem as individual performance. Where expectations remain unmet, work with the person's actual manager and the organization's process. A PM may not have line-management authority. Do not use public comparison, raw ticket counts, or personal speculation as substitutes for constructive feedback.

## Make feedback actionable

Use an observation and impact: “The two agreed reviews did not happen, so verification waited three days.” Ask what happened, then distinguish a capacity conflict from a skill or commitment problem.

Agree a specific next step, such as protected review time, pairing, clearer acceptance, or a smaller commitment. Confirm what success will look like and when you will inspect it. Keep the conversation private and proportionate.

If the person lacks authority to change allocations, resolve that with the resource owner. If the issue persists despite support and clear expectations, use the organization's management process. Respectful feedback and reliable follow-up provide better evidence than labels such as “unmotivated.”
