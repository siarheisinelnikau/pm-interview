# How do you handle conflict and protect sustainable delivery?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#leadership-and-behavioral-questions)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Leadership and Behavioral Questions · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Separate the disagreement from the people involved. Establish shared facts and outcomes, hear the relevant perspectives, and decide at the appropriate level. Use a small experiment when evidence can settle a technical or process disagreement.

Watch for overloaded specialists, repeated emergency work, missed recovery time, and reluctance to raise concerns. Make the cost of interruptions visible and negotiate priorities or staffing. An exceptional recovery effort may be necessary, but repeated overtime is evidence that the plan or system needs changing. Follow up to see whether the intervention improved both delivery and the team's ability to work sustainably.

## Investigate the operating conditions

If engineers and quality specialists repeatedly argue about readiness, inspect whether acceptance examples and verification capacity were agreed early enough. A joint scenario review may resolve ambiguity that escalation alone cannot.

For workload, compare planned capacity with actual interruptions and recovery time. Look for recurring weekend work, concentrated specialist demand, and tasks that only one person can perform. Reduce scope, rebalance support, train backups, or resolve priority conflicts with the relevant owners.

Follow up privately where people may not feel able to raise concerns in a group. Sustainable delivery does not mean every period is comfortable; it means the operating model does not depend on repeated exceptional effort to meet ordinary commitments.
