# What would you do if a sponsor asked you to hide a delay or omit a material risk?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#leadership-and-behavioral-questions)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Leadership and Behavioral Questions · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Establish the facts and clarify the request, then preserve accurate reporting. There is a difference between tailoring detail to an audience and removing information that changes its decisions.

## Respond with evidence and alternatives

Explain what is known, what remains uncertain, and why the omission would mislead. Offer a concise, decision-oriented report: the actual forecast, impact, recovery options, and support needed. Check disputed facts with their owners rather than using a disagreement as grounds to suppress them.

If the request persists, use the organization's governance, ethics, or escalation channels. Keep records factual, proportionate, and appropriately restricted. A PM should not promise a particular legal protection or bypass the established process without understanding the circumstances.

## Example

A sponsor wants a green status before an investment review despite an unbooked certification slot driving the forecast. Report the dependency, conditional forecast, and options. The sponsor may authorize a smaller release or revised investment; changing a color does not resolve the dependency.

The same principle applies to invented acceptance evidence, altered baselines, undisclosed conflicts of interest, and claims made to a customer without support.

## Explain accountability in an interview

Describe the conversation you would have, the information you would verify, the decision needed, and the route for unresolved pressure. Avoid turning the answer into a claim that you alone decide every business risk.

PMI's professional ethics resources emphasize responsibility, respect, fairness, and honesty within their stated scope. Use those principles to support accurate decisions and respectful escalation, rather than treating loyalty to an individual as a reason to misrepresent the project.
