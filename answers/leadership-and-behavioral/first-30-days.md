# What would you do in your first 30 days on an inherited project?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#leadership-and-behavioral-questions)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Leadership and Behavioral Questions · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Establish the objective, current commitments, decision authority, and available evidence. Meet the sponsor, team, users, and critical dependency owners. Review scope, accepted work, forecast, costs, risks, contracts, and operational readiness.

Observe the workflow before changing ceremonies or tools. Identify urgent exposure and correct it, then propose a small set of changes with owners. By the end of the period, aim for a shared factual picture, realistic near-term commitments, and an agreed decision cadence. If the baseline is unreliable, say so and plan how to rebuild it rather than presenting inherited numbers as verified.

## Sequence the work by evidence

In the first week, identify urgent exposure and map authority, commitments, and key people. In the next period, validate accepted progress, cost assumptions, the dependency network, and readiness evidence with their owners. Use the final period to agree improvements and a realistic near-term plan.

Timeboxes are illustrative: a troubled project may need immediate containment, while a large program takes longer to understand. Preserve useful existing practices.

Present a short assessment that distinguishes verified facts, assumptions, unresolved decisions, and recommendations. Agree how the sponsor will resolve material trade-offs. Replacing every tool or ceremony before understanding the system can consume capacity without fixing the actual problem.
