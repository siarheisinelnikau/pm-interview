# How do you close a project and make sure its benefits survive handover?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Confirm acceptance or record unresolved exceptions with owners. Transfer support responsibilities, access, runbooks, contracts, monitoring, and known risks to the receiving organization. Reconcile costs and supplier obligations, archive decisions, and release temporary resources.

Closure should not make benefits ownerless. Schedule an outcome review and specify who can act if adoption or performance disappoints. For a new patient portal, a signed delivery acceptance does not prove that patients can book appointments successfully. Agree who measures that after launch. Record lessons as actionable changes to future work, rather than a list of complaints without owners.

## Test handover, then close

Run a practical acceptance exercise: the support team receives an alert, locates the runbook, obtains the required access, and performs the agreed recovery procedure. Record gaps before declaring operational ownership transferred.

The closure record should distinguish accepted deliverables, outstanding obligations, deferred scope, residual risks, and benefits still to be measured. Give each remaining item a receiving owner and funding source. A list assigned to a dissolved project team is not a handover.

Plan a later benefits review, such as six weeks after rollout, with a named business owner and an agreed action if adoption disappoints. The exact period depends on how soon meaningful outcome evidence can exist.
