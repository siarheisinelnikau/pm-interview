# What belongs in a business case, and when should you revisit it?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Explain the problem, affected users, expected benefits, realistic options, estimated total cost, major uncertainty, and recommendation. Include the option to defer or do nothing. Separate evidence from assumptions and identify who owns each benefit.

Suppose automating claim intake costs 180,000 and is expected to reduce manual handling. Validate transaction volume, actual time saved, adoption, maintenance, and integration costs before presenting a payback claim. Revisit the case when assumptions, regulation, vendor costs, or expected outcomes change. Prior spending alone does not justify continuing; compare the remaining cost and risk with the benefits still achievable.

## Work through the economics

Assume 10,000 claims per month and four minutes of avoidable handling per claim. The theoretical saving is about 667 staff-hours per month. If only 60 percent of claims use the new workflow, it falls to about 400 hours before considering exceptions and support.

That is capacity released, not automatically cash saved. Finance must distinguish reduced overtime, avoided recruitment, faster service, and actual budget reduction. Add ongoing operating costs and a realistic adoption curve before estimating payback.

When costs rise, compare the future alternatives using remaining costs and achievable benefits. Money already spent informs learning and accountability, but does not make an otherwise unjustified continuation worthwhile.
