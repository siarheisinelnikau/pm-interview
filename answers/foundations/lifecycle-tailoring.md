# How would you choose a predictive, adaptive, or hybrid delivery approach?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Choose based on uncertainty, feedback speed, dependency structure, and the cost of change. A predictive plan can fit well-understood work with stable acceptance requirements. An adaptive approach helps when user needs and implementation require learning. A hybrid approach can combine investment gates with incremental delivery.

For example, obtain approval for a clinical deployment boundary, then test workflow increments with clinicians within it. Explicitly define what each gate decides and how changes are handled between gates. Avoid claiming that a regulated environment forbids iteration or that adaptive delivery removes budgets, documentation, and external commitments.

## Tailor controls to uncertainty

A data-center equipment replacement may have a largely known sequence and tightly controlled installation windows. A new clinician-facing workflow may need repeated discovery and usability feedback. A hybrid can authorize investment in stages while delivering and testing software incrementally inside them.

Ask how quickly the team can obtain meaningful feedback, what changes are expensive or irreversible, and which external decisions must happen in advance. Keep evidence and assurance proportionate to the consequence of failure.

Explain the chosen approach operationally: which work is planned in detail now, what remains a forecast, when scope can change, and who approves a new funding boundary. “We use Agile” does not answer these questions.

## Choose a team workflow within that approach

Scrum can fit complex product work where a team can pursue a coherent Sprint Goal and inspect a usable Increment. Kanban can help manage a continuous arrival of work through explicit policies and WIP controls. They can be combined: Scrum does not remove queues, and Kanban does not prevent scheduled planning or reviews.

For a team mixing product changes and urgent support, investigate demand and capacity before choosing a label. Agree how urgent work is handled and how the goal or service expectation remains credible. No framework choice removes external approvals, client responsibilities, or the need for a realistic forecast.
