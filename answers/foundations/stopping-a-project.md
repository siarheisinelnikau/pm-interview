# When should a project be stopped, and how do you close it responsibly?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Recommend stopping when the remaining investment no longer has a credible justification, the intended outcome is no longer needed, or required conditions cannot be met at an acceptable level of exposure. Compare the future alternatives rather than defending the original plan because money has already been spent.

## Make the decision explicit

Separate a temporary hold, a reduced scope, a changed approach, and termination. A hold needs an owner, a review date, and conditions for resuming; otherwise it can consume resources indefinitely.

Present the sponsor or relevant investment authority with the current evidence, remaining costs, achievable benefits, contractual consequences, and options. Include obligations that survive stopping, such as supplier payments, support, data handling, or communication to affected users. The PM supplies a recommendation and coordinates the decision; funding and termination authority follow the actual governance arrangements.

## Example

A planned portal depends on a service that the client will retire. A cheaper integration could still meet the user need, or the benefit may have disappeared entirely. Validate those alternatives before proposing a rescue budget. A falling estimate of remaining work does not help if the result is no longer useful.

## Execute an orderly exit

Agree what happens to partially delivered capabilities, live users, records, access, environments, licenses, and supplier agreements. Confirm ownership of any work or risk retained by the business. Reconcile spending, preserve decisions and required evidence, release resources, and capture lessons with actionable owners.

Report the outcome candidly. A justified early stop can protect value; it should not be presented as a successful delivery of the original scope.
