# How do you establish governance without creating excessive bureaucracy?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Identify the decisions the project needs, their owners, and the thresholds for escalation. Give the team authority for routine choices. Reserve sponsor or steering decisions for material changes to funding, outcomes, scope, and risk exposure.

A small project may need one decision log and a weekly sponsor conversation. A multi-vendor rollout may need formal release and funding gates. Every meeting should have a purpose, inputs, a decision or useful inspection, and follow-up ownership. If a committee receives the same status for weeks without resolving a blocked decision, change the decision path or membership.

## Use explicit tolerances

Illustrative delegation might allow the PM to resequence work within an agreed milestone window, while a funding increase needs sponsor approval and a security exception needs a risk owner. Set actual thresholds with those authorities; do not invent them in a status meeting.

A decision log should record the question, options, evidence, decision, conditions, owner, and date. Distinguish advice from approval and note when a decision expires.

Measure governance by whether decisions arrive early enough to matter. If a supplier choice waits three weeks for a monthly committee, use a delegated decision or an exceptional meeting when authorized. Adding a second status deck will not remove that delay.
