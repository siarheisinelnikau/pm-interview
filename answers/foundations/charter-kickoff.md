# What should be agreed in a project charter and kickoff?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

A useful charter states the objective, sponsor, PM authority, scope boundaries, success measures, constraints, initial milestones, funding assumptions, and major risks. It authorizes the initiative at the level required by the organization.

Use the kickoff to establish a shared understanding: who is involved, how decisions happen, how work and risks become visible, and what happens next. Confirm dependencies and client responsibilities, not just the internal team structure. For example, name the owner and deadline for access to a bank sandbox. A slide deck is insufficient if nobody accepts responsibility for the first critical dependency.

## Leave the kickoff with decisions

For a six-person integration project, useful outputs are a named sponsor, agreed scope boundary, first accepted milestone, client access owner, spending authority, and escalation route. Capture open assumptions with validation dates.

Distinguish authorization from alignment. A kickoff can align people without authorizing funding; a charter can authorize work without ensuring participants understand it. Confirm both using the organization's actual governance process.

A practical first milestone could be “one representative transaction passes through the partner sandbox and its result is verified.” That exposes credentials, mapping, and ownership problems earlier than a milestone called “development started.” Send the decision record to the people who must act on it.
