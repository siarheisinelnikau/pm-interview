# How do you plan adoption and respond to resistance to a new system or process?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#project-management-foundations)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Project Management Foundations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Plan the change in work as well as the software. Identify which roles, decisions, responsibilities, incentives, and handoffs will change, and involve the people affected before rollout.

## Understand resistance before treating it

A person may resist because the proposed workflow adds work, removes useful autonomy, has accessibility problems, threatens a professional responsibility, or has been poorly explained. Observation and conversation help distinguish those issues from a training gap. Do not assume resistance is irrational or that a message from the sponsor resolves it.

Agree a future operating model with business owners. Name who will update procedures, permissions, staffing, training, and support. Give local managers and users time to participate.

## Build readiness into the plan

| Area | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- |
| Process | People understand the new handoffs and exception paths |
| Capability | Representative users can complete real tasks, including recovery |
| Capacity | Workload during transition and support demand are covered |
| Ownership | Managers can resolve local issues and sustain the change |
| Adoption | Usage and outcomes are measured with appropriate segmentation |

Training attendance is an activity measure. Successful completion of a representative task is stronger evidence of readiness.

## Example

A hospital releases digital intake, but nurses still transcribe information into another system. More training may not fix the missing integration or responsibility for checking the record. Investigate the actual workflow and change the process or scope with its owners.

Use a supported pilot, feedback channels, and explicit expansion criteria. After launch, inspect sustained adoption, task success, workload, and the intended benefit. Give unresolved improvements a business owner; adoption does not become self-sustaining merely because the project closes.
