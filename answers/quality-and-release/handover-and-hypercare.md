# What belongs in operational handover and a time-bounded hypercare period?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#quality-release-and-operations)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Quality, Release, and Operations · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Transfer service ownership, support contacts, runbooks, dashboards, access, known issues, vendor obligations, and recovery procedures. Agree how incidents are classified and who can make urgent changes. Validate that the receiving team can perform key operations.

Hypercare is a defined period of additional support with staffing, reporting, and exit criteria. For example, exit may require stable critical journeys, no unresolved severe incidents, and an accepted backlog of remaining issues. Avoid ending solely because a date arrived or extending forever because nobody accepted ownership. Leave improvement work with named owners and funded capacity.

## Define a practical exit

Agree who owns support, monitoring, incident decisions, access administration, supplier contact, and the backlog of remaining work. Test the receiving team's ability to perform the critical tasks rather than only obtaining a signature.

Possible hypercare exit criteria include enough stable observation, no unresolved critical service-impacting issues, accepted ownership of remaining items, and a demonstrated response to representative alerts. Set the actual criteria with the service owner.

If exit criteria fail, choose a funded extension, reduced exposure, or another response with accountable owners. Preserve the distinction between extra project support and ongoing warranty or service obligations in the actual agreement.
