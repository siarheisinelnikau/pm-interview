# How do an RFI, an RFP response, a proposal, and a statement of work differ?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#presales-and-solutions---adjacent)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Presales and Solutions - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

An RFI gathers information; an RFP requests a response to a stated need under the buyer's process. A proposal presents the offered solution and commercial approach. A statement of work defines the agreed delivery scope and responsibilities within the relevant contract.

In practice, document names and legal effects vary. Check assumptions, deliverables, acceptance, exclusions, client obligations, milestones, pricing basis, and change handling with the commercial owner. Ensure the final agreement matches the delivery team's understanding rather than relying on an earlier sales presentation.

## Trace the final agreement

Map each mandatory buyer requirement to the offered response, evidence, exception, and delivery owner. Clarify which proposal statements are incorporated into the signed agreement; document hierarchy and legal effect depend on that agreement.

A useful SOW defines deliverables, acceptance, exclusions, client inputs, milestones, commercial basis, and change handling. It should state assumptions such as access dates rather than hiding them in the estimate worksheet.

For terminology, FAR offers US federal examples of information requests and performance work statements. Use them as examples of procurement practice, not a universal template for every private-sector engagement.
