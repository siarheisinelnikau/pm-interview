# How do you scope a proof of concept so it supports a buying decision?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#presales-and-solutions---adjacent)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Presales and Solutions - Adjacent · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

State the uncertain hypothesis, representative scenario, environment, timebox, success evidence, and decision owner. Define what the proof of concept excludes and what additional work production use requires.

A demo may illustrate a workflow; a proof of concept should test a specific uncertainty, such as whether an integration can support the required data. Record security and data constraints before using client information. Close with results, limitations, and next steps. A successful test does not automatically establish scalability, production readiness, or a fixed implementation price.

## Close the uncertainty, then decide

A two-week integration POC might test whether representative records can be exchanged under the partner's security model. Define sample data, expected behavior, failure cases, and the evidence needed for a feasibility decision.

List production gaps explicitly: scale, monitoring, support, migration, assurance, and any applicable obligations. A prototype with a mocked dependency cannot establish that the real partner integration works.

At the end, recommend proceed, investigate further, change approach, or stop. Price the remaining production work using the actual findings. The POC earns its value by changing confidence in a decision, not by producing a polished demonstration.
