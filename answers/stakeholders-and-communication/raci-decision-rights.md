# When is a RACI useful, and what should it clarify?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#stakeholders-and-communication)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Stakeholders and Communication · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

A RACI distinguishes people who do work (Responsible), answer for its result (Accountable), provide input (Consulted), and need updates (Informed). Define its scope and agree it with the people involved.

For a release, distinguish producing evidence, accepting business readiness, approving deployment, and accepting residual risk. A single “release owner” row can hide those separate decisions. Keep the matrix focused on areas where responsibility is unclear, and resolve conflicting authority directly. Having someone's name in a table does not ensure they have time or authority to act. Update the operating agreements as well.

## Use a decision-sized row

For an access-control exception, engineering may supply implementation evidence, security may assess exposure, and an authorized risk owner may be accountable for a permissible decision. Communicating the outcome is a separate task.

A common RACI convention gives each row one accountable owner. If several people appear accountable, split the row into distinct decisions or clarify the governance rule; committees can have a formal collective role where the organization defines one.

Ask whether the named person has the authority, information, and availability to act. Add deputies or escalation times for critical decisions. A tidy matrix cannot compensate for a sponsor who has not actually delegated authority.
