# How do you influence a decision when you have no direct authority?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#stakeholders-and-communication)

**Level:** Senior · **Topic:** Stakeholders and Communication · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Understand the other party's objectives and constraints, then build a shared view of the facts. Offer options that connect the project need to their priorities. Make the request concrete, including effort, deadline, and consequences.

If an infrastructure team cannot provide an environment, investigate queue position, prerequisites, and possible smaller requests. A limited environment or reserved validation window might unblock the critical path. Confirm the agreement in the normal decision record and recognize shared ownership. If negotiation cannot resolve a material conflict, use the agreed escalation path. Repeating “the project is important” does not resolve competing demands.

## Offer a concrete exchange

Instead of asking an infrastructure team to “prioritize us,” ask for a two-hour validation window with a prepared configuration and named engineer. Offer to reduce setup effort, use an existing environment, or move a lower-priority request when authorized.

Clarify the other team's constraints and the person who can trade priorities across both areas. Document the agreement, evidence of completion, and escalation trigger.

Influence also requires credibility: keep your own prerequisites and avoid repeatedly labeling preventable late requests as urgent. Where incentives or authority conflict, a sponsor-level decision may be necessary. The PM's role is to expose that conflict with options, not obscure it through personal pressure.
