# Two senior stakeholders want conflicting priorities. How do you resolve it?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#stakeholders-and-communication)

**Level:** Middle · **Topic:** Stakeholders and Communication · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Clarify the underlying outcomes, constraints, urgency, and consequences of each request. Establish who owns the priority decision and which criteria were agreed. Make the trade-off visible rather than asking the team to satisfy incompatible commitments.

For example, compare revenue timing, operational risk, effort, and dependencies for a customer feature and a resilience improvement. Explore sequencing or a smaller scope if it preserves the important outcomes. Facilitate the decision with the product or sponsor authority, record it, and communicate what moves. Escalate unresolved authority conflicts; informal side agreements can create a plan that nobody can actually deliver.

## Make the incompatible demand explicit

Suppose sales needs a new onboarding path for a signed customer while operations needs work on repeated failures. Ask about revenue timing, customer harm, reliability exposure, and the effort needed for each option.

A short decision table can compare full feature, smaller feature plus reliability work, or delayed feature. Identify non-negotiable obligations before scoring discretionary value. Agree who decides when product, funding, and risk authority are split.

Once decided, communicate displaced work and the conditions that would reopen the decision. Continuing to promise both original dates privately creates a conflict the team cannot solve through effort alone.
