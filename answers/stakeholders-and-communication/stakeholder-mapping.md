# How do you identify stakeholders and decide who needs to be involved?

[Back to questions](../../README.md#stakeholders-and-communication)

**Level:** Junior · **Topic:** Stakeholders and Communication · **Reviewed:** 2026-10-30

## Answer

Map who funds, uses, approves, operates, influences, or is affected by the change. Consider decision power, interest, impact, and knowledge. Include people outside the reporting hierarchy, such as clinical staff, support agents, security reviewers, and customer operations.

Plan engagement according to the decision or learning needed. A sponsor needs concise trade-offs; frontline users need opportunities to validate workflows. Revisit the map when scope changes. A project can fail despite executive support if the people expected to adopt or operate it were never involved. Stakeholder mapping should lead to specific conversations and owners.

## Map impact as well as influence

A power–interest grid is a useful starting view, but people with little organizational power can bear substantial consequences. Include patients, accessibility users, frontline staff, and support teams according to the project's actual impact.

For each stakeholder group, record the decision or learning needed, engagement method, owner, and timing. For example, nurses validate handoffs before workflow acceptance; operations review recovery before release; the sponsor resolves a funding trade-off.

Validate the map with people who understand the environment. Revisit it when a site, supplier, data use, or operating process changes. A communication plan that only distributes status does not provide the participation needed to validate a solution.
